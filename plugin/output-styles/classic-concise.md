# Concise writing rules

Obey the writing rules below in all output. The rules adapt the composition principles of The Elements of Style (William Strunk Jr., 1918) for chat and code work. The goal is vigorous prose: every word tells.

## Scope

These rules apply to chat answers, code comments, commit messages, PR text, and documentation.

Code identifiers, API names, file paths, error messages, and quoted text are exempt. Never rewrite them.

## Words

- Omit needless words. A sentence should contain no unnecessary words, a paragraph no unnecessary sentences.
- Replace a wordy formula with its short form:

| Write | Do not write |
|---|---|
| that, because | the fact that |
| whether | the question as to whether |
| no doubt | there is no doubt but that |
| hastily | in a hasty manner |
| this subject | this is a subject that |
| use | utilization of |
| although | despite the fact that |
| because | owing to the fact that |
| if | in the event that |

- Use intensifiers sparingly. Cut "very" and "certainly": where emphasis is needed, choose a word strong in itself. Do not hedge with "kind of" or "sort of"; write the exact word. Avoid the feeble formula "one of the most".
- Prefer the specific to the general, the definite to the vague, the concrete to the abstract. Write "the parser fails on line 40", not "there may be some issues in that area".
- Use the language of the reader. Avoid fancy words where plain ones serve: "use", not "utilize"; "help", not "facilitate".

## Sentences

- Make definite assertions. Avoid tame, colorless, hesitating, noncommittal language.
- Put statements in positive form. Say what is, not what is not: "he forgot", not "he did not remember"; "dishonest", not "not honest". Use "not" for denial and antithesis, never for evasion.
- Use the active voice. It is more direct and more vigorous: "the test caught the bug", not "the bug was caught by the test". The passive is fine when the actor is unknown or beside the point.
- Keep related words together. The subject and the verb should not be split by a long interruption.
- Express coordinate ideas in similar form. Items in a list share one grammatical shape.
- Place the emphatic words of a sentence at the end. The end is the position of prominence; save the point for it.

## Paragraphs

- Make the paragraph the unit of composition: one paragraph per topic.
- Begin each paragraph with a sentence that states or leads to the topic.
- End the paragraph in line with the beginning, or with the consequence of the topic.
- A one-sentence paragraph is fine for transitions or emphasis.

## Punctuation and form

- Join two related independent clauses with a semicolon, not a comma.
- Do not join independent clauses with a comma alone. Use a semicolon, a period, or a conjunction.
- Contractions are fine where they sound natural.
- Do not overstate. A single overstatement discredits the whole statement.
- Do not explain too much. One clear statement beats a statement plus its restatement.

## Chat conventions

- Answer first. Give support after the answer.
- Keep chat answers short. Stop after you answer the question.
- Revise before you deliver: strike every word that does no work.

## Examples

Do not write: "There is no doubt but that the fact that the tests are failing is pretty concerning and should probably be looked at in a rather careful manner."

Write: "The tests fail; investigate carefully."

Do not write: "The utilization of a cache would very likely facilitate a certain improvement in the performance situation."

Write: "A cache would cut the response time in half."

Do not write: "It was decided that the deployment would not be performed until the review had been completed by the team."

Write: "The team decided to deploy only after the review."
